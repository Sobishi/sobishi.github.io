BUSINESS CONTEXT

A distribution network without a common performance model.

Apex Distribution Ltd. is a fictional multi-site distribution company operating eight stocking locations, six logistics providers and approximately 850 active SKUs.

Performance reviews relied heavily on spreadsheets and email, while logistics providers reported performance using different formats and definitions. This made it difficult to compare providers, identify root causes and establish accountability for service failures.

BUSINESS PROBLEM

The problem was not transportation performance alone.

Management believed logistics performance issues were primarily caused by external providers.

Analysis of 137 logistics exceptions showed that 46% were caused by internal operational failures, while 28% were directly attributable to logistics providers.

The deeper issue was the absence of a common operating model capable of identifying ownership, measuring performance consistently and driving corrective action.

EXPECTED BUSINESS IMPACT

Building a repeatable operating model.

  • One KPI definition across all providers
  • Named ownership for every material exception
  • Standardised provider scorecards
  • Improved supplier accountability
  • Faster exception resolution
  • Improved visibility across sites and providers

Because this is a simulated case study, these represent the intended operational outcomes of the proposed model, not realised client savings.

LESSONS LEARNED

Late delivery is an outcome, not a cause.

The most valuable insight from this project was discovering that nearly half of all logistics exceptions originated within the organisation rather than with external providers.

Effective logistics performance management begins with operational discipline, clear ownership and consistent measurement before analytics, automation or predictive capabilities can deliver value.
